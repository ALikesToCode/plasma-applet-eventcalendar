Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-11-02

Summary

Plasma Event Calendar is a free, open-source calendar widget for the KDE Plasma desktop. It is maintained by ALikesToCode on GitHub. The widget runs entirely on your computer. It has no server of its own. Your Google Calendar and Google Tasks content travels directly between your computer and Google, and the developer never receives it.

Plasma Event Calendar's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Google Calendar and Google Tasks

The widget does not connect to Google unless you sign in from its settings (Settings > Google Calendar). Signing in opens Google's own sign-in page in your web browser, where Google shows you the access the widget is asking for. You can decline.

Data the widget accesses

  • Your list of calendars: names, colours, time zones and your access level, so you can choose which calendars to show.
  • Events in the calendars you choose, as Google returns them, such as the title, time, location, description, attendees and video call links.
  • Your task lists and the tasks in the lists you choose.

How the widget uses this data

  • To show your events and tasks in the Plasma panel, the calendar popup and the agenda.
  • To make the changes you ask for in the widget: adding, editing or deleting an event, and adding, editing, completing or deleting a task. The widget never changes your data on its own.
  • To refresh this information from time to time, and when you press Refresh.

The widget does not use Google data for advertising. It does not sell it, and it does not use it to develop, improve or train generalized artificial intelligence or machine learning models. The developer does not receive or read your calendar or task data.

Where the data is stored

  • Your Google access and refresh tokens are stored in your desktop keyring: Secret Service (for example GNOME Keyring or KeePassXC) or KWallet. They are not written to the widget configuration file.
  • If you set up your own Google OAuth client, the client ID and client secret you enter are saved in the widget configuration file with your other settings. After you sign in, a copy of the secret is also kept in your keyring with your tokens.
  • An account label, your list of calendars and task lists (IDs, names, colours, time zones and access level) and which of them you chose are stored in the Plasma widget configuration file, ~/.config/plasma-org.kde.plasma.desktop-appletsrc.
  • Events and tasks are kept in memory only while Plasma is running. They are not written to disk, and they are downloaded again when the widget refreshes.

Sharing, transfer and disclosure of Google user data

Plasma Event Calendar does not sell or rent Google user data. The developer does not receive your Google Calendar or Google Tasks data, access tokens or refresh tokens. The widget does not share, transfer or disclose Google user data to advertisers, data brokers or analytics providers. Its data recipients and purposes are limited to those described here:

  • Google: the widget connects directly to Google's OAuth service, Google Calendar API and Google Tasks API over HTTPS. Sign-in codes and tokens are exchanged with Google to authorize access. Calendar and task requests, including the event or task data you ask the widget to create, edit, complete or delete, are sent to Google to keep your account in sync. Google's handling of this data is covered by the Google Privacy Policy.
  • Your desktop: KDE Plasma displays the calendars, events and tasks on your computer. Your desktop keyring stores the sign-in tokens locally, as described above. This does not send them to the developer or to a developer-operated server.
  • People with access to a shared calendar: if you create or edit an event in a shared Google calendar, people who already have access may see it according to your Google Calendar sharing settings. The widget does not change who has access to your calendars.
  • Websites you choose to open: if you click a link in an event or task, including a video meeting link, the widget opens it in your browser. The destination service receives the link's URL, which may contain an event or meeting identifier, and your browser's request. Its own privacy policy applies. The widget does not send your Google access or refresh tokens with these links.

Google Calendar and Google Tasks content, access tokens and refresh tokens are not sent to GitHub Pages, weather providers or iCalendar feed hosts. These services are separate from the Google integration. The widget and this website do not use analytics or tracking. The OAuth helper's handling of a sign-in code is described below.

Signing in

After you sign in, Google sends a one-time authorization code to a temporary address on your own computer (http://127.0.0.1). The widget exchanges that code with Google for tokens. If you paste a redirect URL into the OAuth helper's form on this website instead, the code is read by JavaScript in your browser and is sent only to the widget's listener on your own computer. If you use the hosted redirect option, or open the website with a sign-in code in its URL, GitHub Pages receives that URL, including the code, when serving the page. The helper then forwards the code to the widget on your computer. It does not exchange the code for tokens on the website. GitHub Pages may log website requests, including your IP address and request URL, as described in the GitHub Privacy Statement.

Removing your data and revoking access

  • In the widget, open Settings > Google Calendar, select the account and press Remove. This deletes its tokens from your keyring and its calendar list from the widget configuration. Removing the widget from your panel removes its configuration but leaves the tokens in your keyring, so remove the account first, or delete the keyring entries whose names start with plasma-applet-eventcalendar.
  • To revoke the widget's access on Google's side, go to myaccount.google.com/connections, choose Plasma Event Calendar and remove its access. The widget then stops receiving data until you sign in again.

What the developer can see

The Google Cloud console shows the developer aggregate numbers for the widget's OAuth client: how many API requests were made and how many failed, by API method and HTTP status code. These numbers are totals for all users. They do not include who made a request or any calendar or task content.

Other online services

These features are off by default. When you turn one on, the widget connects directly to that service, which can see your IP address and the request.

  • Weather: if you choose a city, the widget downloads forecasts from OpenWeatherMap (privacy policy) or Environment and Climate Change Canada (weather.gc.ca), depending on the provider you choose.
  • iCalendar feeds: if you add an iCal URL, the widget downloads it from the server that hosts it.

Downloads

If you install the widget from the KDE Store (for example with Get New Widgets in Plasma), the KDE Store privacy policy applies to the download. If you install it from GitHub, GitHub's privacy statement applies. If you get it from another source, such as an AUR package, that service's own privacy policy applies to the download. The developer can see how many times each release was downloaded, but not who downloaded it.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the new version will be published on this page with a new date. Questions and requests can be raised as an issue on GitHub.
